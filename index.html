How this order is worked out

With one night left, the useful question isn't what's on the list — it's what earns the most marks per minute. Every structure gets a score:

marks at risk = chance it gets tagged × chance you'd miss it right now

Chance it gets tagged is my estimate, shown as a tier. A expect it, B likely, C plausible, and unflagged terms still carry a baseline. I based this on what a pointer can rest on unambiguously, what the objectives sheet also ties to a function or to the oxygenated/deoxygenated question, and the fact that left/right and superior/middle/inferior pairs are perennial tags.

Chance you'd miss it comes from your own pins. A structure you've never located anywhere is one you can't answer at all, so it scores far higher than one you've already handled.

Near-twins get a boost. Superior vs inferior vena cava, epicardium vs endocardium, epiglottis vs glottis — pairs like these are where practical marks actually leak, so anything with a look-alike is pushed up the list.

This is a prediction, not inside information. I did not write your exam and I have not seen it. A low tier means "less probable than the others", never "safe to skip". If your professor stressed something in lab, trust that over this.
